Headline: One Sentence Naming the Product, Your Move, and the Number It Moved.

Section type: Context

H2 plus one paragraph plus an optional wide figure. Sets the scene in three or four sentences: what the product is, who uses it, what was at stake when you arrived. From markdown this is a plain ## Heading followed by a paragraph. One idea per section — if a second paragraph creeps in, it probably belongs in its own section.

Section type: Problem

A one-paragraph lead naming the core difficulty, followed by a split row: evidence on the left, the breakdown on the right.

  • Named pain: each bullet opens with a bold label and states one concrete, verifiable difficulty — with a number where one exists.
  • Constraint: the second bullet is usually the constraint that made the pain hard to fix: team size, tech debt, deadline, platform.

Section type: Process

A lead sentence naming your approach, then alternating bullet-and-figure pairs. Each pair is one method: what you did, what it showed.

  • Method one: what you actually did, stated as an action with an object — audited N reviews, benchmarked X, mapped Y. The figure below it shows the artifact.
  • Method two: repeat the pattern. Two or three methods is the ceiling — more reads as a checklist, and checklists are the rejection flag this template exists to avoid.

Section type: Decision

A bolded thesis sentence naming the decision. Then a split row per decision: the artifact on one side, action and result on the other.

  • Action: what you changed and what you traded away to get it — the trade-off is the credible part.
  • Result: the measured consequence of this decision alone, not of the whole project.

Repeat per decision, two or three times. Include the attempt that failed: what you tried first, why it did not survive contact with users.

  • What failed: the honest account of the first attempt.
  • What it taught: the insight that shaped the shipped version.

Section type: Outcome

One paragraph connecting the work to the change, then the metrics as bullets. Every number carries its period and its baseline — a number without a baseline is decoration.

  • Headline metric: the one from the home card, with the period it covers and what it was before.
  • Second metric: supporting number with honest attribution — your share of it versus the team\'s and the market\'s.
  • Third metric: the ceiling. Past three, numbers start diluting each other.
